[Slow neo-Verdi operatic monologue for bass-baritone, Bryn Terfel-inspired gravitas, restrained orchestra, ancient sacred atmosphere, no bombastic climax]

[Intro — Low strings, distant wind, soft horn among standing stones]
The night still knows my name.
The stones recall footsteps older than kings.
Here the wind does not lie.
Here silence preserves what men forget.

[Verse 1 — Calm, reflective]
I walked along vanished shores,
when Avalon still spoke to blood.
I saw the fire sleep beneath the hill
and the waters part without fear.

Now the torches burn colder.
Now men pray to other heavens.
And yet…
among these stones something remains.

[Orchestral breath — low woodwinds, restrained strings]

[Verse 2 — Deeper, darker]
It is not power.
Power passes like iron in the rain.
It is guardianship.
It is listening to the earth
when even the gods are silent.

Many wanted to master the mystery.
Many have called the fog by its name.
The fog has forgotten them all.

[Bridge — quiet rise]
I still hear the breathing beneath the rock.
Slow.
Ancient.
Like the last coal beneath the ashes of the world.

As long as that breathing remains,
all is not lost.

[Verse 3 — weary but strong]
My hands tremble more than winter.
The years sit heavy in my bones.
But here…
among moss, rain, and stone,
the earth still remembers who I am.

And as long as the stars return above the lake,
as long as the sea brings mist to the shores,
someone must keep watch.

[Short orchestral passage — horn and low strings only]

[Final section — solemn and intimate]
Not for glory.
Not for kingdoms.
Kingdoms pass.

But certain doors,
once closed,
must never be opened again.

[Closing — almost spoken through music]
This is why I stay.
This is why I listen.
This is why I guard the silence
where the stones speak.

[Outro — distant horn fading into wind]
